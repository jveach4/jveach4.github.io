01 / The opportunity

Make rigorous assessment work easier to execute.

Organizations pursue opportunities while balancing risk, cost, resources, and expected benefit. Risk assessments provide a structured foundation for those decisions, but the first assessment cycle can demand substantial time from both practitioners and the business.

The design starts with information an organization already has—even when it lacks a mature risk register or control library. Documents, stakeholder accounts, and existing records become inputs to structured analysis, targeted follow-up, and reviewable decisions. Gathering evidence, interviewing stakeholders, evaluating controls, and preparing reporting are all opportunities to reduce avoidable effort.

The development question: How can AI reduce that burden and accelerate assessment work while preserving evidence, professional judgment, and accountability?

My contribution

Domain expertise translated into product decisions.

I defined the assessment methodology and translated practitioner judgment into product requirements, decision rules, evidence expectations, and approval controls.

I determined where AI could organize, analyze, draft, and challenge; where software should calculate and enforce rules; and where accountable people must decide.

Across successive prototypes, I challenged and refined the operating model: separating management representations from corroborated evidence, remediation completion from effectiveness, and AI proposals from approved decisions.

I directed AI-assisted documentation and implementation, reviewed the resulting designs, and shaped traceability, testing, and release criteria.

Work to date includes methodology and product specifications, a documented database foundation, development and testing records, and a standalone synthetic demo. Business benefits remain to be measured.

02 / Design priorities

Make the reasoning as reviewable as the recommendation.

01

Evidence before confidence.

The design pairs AI proposals with rationale, source references, missing information, and contradictions. It distinguishes corroborated evidence from management representations and insufficient support, giving reviewers a practical basis for challenge.

02

Human judgment as a control.

People can accept, modify, reject, or investigate recommendations. Material decisions are intended to preserve the original proposal, final judgment, rationale, attribution, and history. Approval responsibility remains with accountable people.

03

Govern the decision rules.

AI assists analysis; deterministic software calculates scores and checks workflow requirements. Methodology, thresholds, and governance triggers are explicit and subject to change control.

What this work demonstrates: the ability to turn experience into a usable operating model, translate it into system requirements, critically review AI-assisted work, and preserve assurance discipline throughout development.

04 / A deeper look

From professional judgment to explicit requirements.

These summaries explain the design logic and the boundaries of the current demonstration. Selected standards connections are documented below, with the scope and evidence for each claim.

AI and human responsibilities

The intended operating model separates AI proposals from accountable human decisions. Review is meant to be supported by information at the point of decision.

AI proposesPrepare an initial assessment, rationale, and evidence references; identify missing information.
Human evaluatesInspect the basis, challenge assumptions, and seek clarification where needed.
Human decidesAccept or revise the assessment and document the reason for the decision.
System preservesIntended design: retain the proposal, human decision, attribution, and version history.

In M022, proposals are seeded examples and decisions are stored only in memory. The broader database records address decision lineage, versioning, and tenant authorization; those controls are not connected to M022. Their documented development checks do not establish production readiness.

Risk scoring and evidence

Impact and likelihood are each assessed on a five-point scale. Their average determines inherent risk. We then average inherent risk with the Control Environment level to calculate residual risk, subject to the documented cap and no-controls exception.

CalculationMethod
Inherent risk(Impact + Likelihood) ÷ 2
Residual risk(Inherent risk + Control Environment level) ÷ 2
Control direction1 Highly Effective · 2 Effective · 3 Moderately Effective · 4 Ineffective · 5 Highly Ineffective or Absent
Cap and exceptionResidual risk cannot exceed inherent risk unless Level 5 explicitly represents no controls in place.

Worked example: Impact 4 and Likelihood 3 produce 3.50—High inherent risk. Control Environment Level 3 produces 3.25—Moderate residual risk.

Rating bands: Very Low 1.00–1.74; Low 1.75–2.74; Moderate 2.75–3.49; High 3.50–4.49; Very High 4.50–5.00.

The review copy of the demo now follows these calculations and thresholds. Evidence references remain synthetic labels, and scores demonstrate the workflow rather than completed professional conclusions. Rationale is required; its substantive quality is not automatically validated.

Scope definition and change control

Development requirements define scope as a structured record with objectives, boundaries, context, and accountable review. AI may propose scope elements; a practitioner can accept, modify, reject, or investigate them.

The intended design preserves approved scope and handles material changes through a new version or amendment. This illustrates how an assessment practice becomes a data and workflow requirement.

Basis: reviewed Scope Record Requirements specification, September 2026. This is a high-level summary; the internal development record is not included. Scope versioning is not implemented in the M022 standalone demo.

From information gathering to accountable action

The methodology spans scope and context, requirements, risk identification, impact and likelihood, controls and Control Environment evaluation, residual risk, appetite and tolerance, treatment, approvals, reporting, and reassessment.

AI-assisted evidence review and interview preparation are designed to surface gaps, inconsistencies, and questions that matter to the specific assessment. Interviews remain human-led in the initial operating model.

Reusable risk, control, evidence, and action records support multiple assessment contexts. The design links information to the conclusions it supports, rather than treating an uploaded document as proof of every related assertion.

Basis: methodology and automation assessments, Evidence/Human-in-the-Loop framework, shared platform architecture, and practitioner review through V3. These describe the intended operating model; M022 demonstrates only selected review interactions.

Three practitioner decisions that shaped the product
DecisionWhy it matters
Evaluate the control environment collectively.Individual control ratings alone do not establish how controls work together to address a risk. The methodology calls for explicit professional judgment about coverage and effectiveness.
Do not automatically reduce risk when an action is completed.Implementation, validation, and operating effectiveness answer different questions. Action completion is an assertion to evaluate; any revised risk conclusion needs its own basis.
Preserve the approved assessment when circumstances change.Versioned reassessment and comparison preserve what was known and decided at the time. New information should not silently rewrite the historical record.

These decisions are documented in practitioner reviews and reflected in related database requirements and migration records. They illustrate domain judgment, not measured production outcomes.

Risk tolerance, treatment, and continuing oversight

The design connects risk conclusions to permitted treatment choices, accountable owners, approval requirements, and follow-up. A tolerance breach triggers evaluation and governance; it does not automatically dictate a single treatment or make every risk unacceptable.

Risk-owner sign-off and additional approvals follow defined responsibilities and triggers. Management action records address completion, independent validation, rejection and rework, cancellation, and carry-forward. Approved baselines and versioned reassessments support a defensible comparison of historical decisions with current conditions.

Basis: treatment/governance rules and M010–M018 development records. Historical test evidence varies by migration and includes retrospective reconstruction; it is not a blanket certification of the complete system.

Standards and regulatory traceability

The documented design maps to selected risk management practices in ISO 31000:2018, NIST SP 800-30 Rev. 1, and COSO ERM 2017. Defined human oversight also relates to selected NIST AI Risk Management Framework 1.0 outcomes.

The mapping connects scope, evidence, risk analysis, treatment, accountable review, reporting, and reassessment to supporting development records. ISO coverage is currently at theme level; NIST tasks and selected COSO principles have more specific references.

The source scoring methodology references NIST SP 800-30, Appendix I. The five-point averaging formulas are a tailored methodology, not formulas prescribed by NIST. Alignment is stated for specific design elements and does not imply certification, endorsement, or complete framework implementation.

Technical implementation

The supplied prototype consists of HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. Six view functions render the overview, assessments, risk register, risk review, approvals, and management report.

Interactions update an in-memory state object and recalculate displayed risk scores. There is no database connection, live AI service, persistent storage, or authenticated user session in this demo.

The portfolio embeds that supplied demonstration in an isolated frame. This review copy corrects the supplied demo’s scoring to the source methodology, including scale direction, rating bands, cap, and explicit no-controls exception. Styles and scripts are inlined to make the preview self-contained. The original ZIP is retained separately.

Development discipline and validation boundaries

The project records include requirements-to-design traceability, change records, security review, test evidence, technical debt, and release gates. Negative tests examine whether invalid relationships, unauthorized changes, or premature workflow transitions are rejected.

Migration closeouts document specific development scenarios, with some historical evidence reconstructed retrospectively. Later security reviews identify unresolved work, and prepared tests are distinguished from executed results.

This demonstrates an approach to controlled development and transparent evidence. It does not establish that the complete platform is secure, production-ready, or independently certified.

Demo defect resolution and validation

The supplied v0.1.1 release documents a rendering failure caused by incorrect view-dispatch references. The correction points the assessment and risk routes to their view functions.

Package-reported checks: JavaScript syntax, simulated-DOM rendering of all six views, and ZIP integrity. The package explicitly does not claim real Edge end-to-end validation.

Preview preparation checks: ZIP integrity, source syntax, route rendering, rationale validation, score saving, approval, and reopening were checked using a simulated DOM. Portfolio anchor targets and self-contained demo assets were checked programmatically.

Desktop portfolio presentation has been visually reviewed. Comprehensive mobile, accessibility, and end-to-end interaction testing remains outstanding. These checks do not establish production security or readiness.

How I would evaluate the intended benefits

Further evaluation should compare manual and AI-assisted completion time on equivalent assessment tasks, review evidence support for recommendations, and examine the quality of human corrections.

Useful measures include reviewer effort, unsupported recommendations, material omissions, revision frequency, and decision-record completeness. These are proposed evaluation measures—not results achieved by this prototype.
